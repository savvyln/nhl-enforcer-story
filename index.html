The Disappearing Playoff Enforcer

Thirteen playoff fights in 2022-23. Two in 2024-25.

A look at the fading post-season brawl, and the players who still drop the mitts.

Fight counts from HockeyFights.com's NHL postseason logs, 2021-22 through 2025-26.

The icebreaker

There is a spectrum of hockey fans: those who hate fighting, and those who love it. We're focusing on those who love it.

For these fight fanatics, the best time of year is the playoffs. Sixteen teams a season face off across the five postseasons from 2021-22 to 2025-26, in hundreds of games.

In all of them, fight fans have witnessed just fights. Nine in the first year, thirteen in the second, then four, two, and four.

Dance, donnybrook, drop the mitts, fisticuffs, have a go, puttin' on the foil, snarl, tilt: whatever you call it, the quintessential playoff brawl is vanishing before our eyes.

The heavyweight mirage

If you clicked through the teams, you likely noticed a glaring absence: the heavyweights.

The modern NHL still has feared, dedicated enforcers like Mathieu Olivier, Ryan Reaves, Nicolas Deslauriers, and Arber Xhekaj. But when the playoffs arrive, they stay quiet.

The accidental gladiators

If the dedicated heavyweights aren't throwing punches, who is? The modern post-season fight is no longer a staged bout between policemen. It is a spontaneous clash between everyday players whose emotions boil over.

What this can't tell you

It is incomplete. Three 2022-23 fights are not in this dataset yet, so the analysis covers fights, not .

Fan votes are opinions. The "winner" and rating on each fight come from site visitors, not from the league.

It counts fights, not causes. Rule changes, how rosters are built, and a faster game are all common explanations for the decline. None is tested here, and the log does not show who started a fight or where on the ice it happened.
