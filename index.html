Do teams that fight more win more?

The road to the cup has been described by sports author George Vecsey as, "“War of attrition, war of wills. That's what the Stanley Cup playoffs are - more intense, more physical and more prolonged than the playoffs of any other sport.”

"War of attrition, war of wills." — George Vecsey

You can see this action clearly in footage traced back to at least 1925. The grit players bring to the ice determines who gets to lift Lord Stanley. But do fights actually get the W?

We investigated this question across five seasons of Stanley Cup Playoffs, between 2021 to 2026, using game data from NHL EDGE and fights logged on hockeyfights.com.

    The Icebreaker

    There is a spectrum of hockey fans: those who hate fighting, and those who love it. For these fight fanatics, the best time of year is the playoffs. Sixteen teams a season face off across the five postseasons from 2021-22 to 2025-26, in hundreds of games.

    In all of them, fight fans have witnessed just fights. Nine in the first year, thirteen in the second, then four, two, and four.

    Dance, donnybrook, drop the mitts, fisticuffs, have a go, puttin' on the foil, snarl, tilt: whatever you call it, the quintessential playoff brawl is vanishing before our eyes.

    Each dot is one fight. Hover, tap, or tab to a dot to see who fought, and click it to flip up both players' trading cards. This project has full details on all fights HockeyFights.com lists for these five postseasons.

    Who dropped the mitts?

    So who is involved? This rink puts every team that took part in a playoff fight on the ice. Click any team logo to flip it and reveal the players who actually dropped the mitts.

    The bigger the logo, the more fights that team was in. Your pick carries through the charts that follow.

    Now compare the two halves of the ice. Eastern Conference teams show up in the fight log, against only from the West. Each conference sends eight teams to the playoffs every spring, so the East's longer list means more of its clubs were getting in, and getting into it.

    That may be a sign of a more competitive East, where the playoff field keeps turning over. The West's shorter list could signal the opposite: a less competitive conference, or one controlled by a select few teams. Two clubs, the , account for of the West's spots in the log.

    Position on this rink shows conference, not where fights happened. Eastern teams sit left of center ice and Western teams sit right. The season chapter above places each fight by zone.

    The Heavyweight Mirage

    If you clicked through the teams, you likely noticed a glaring absence: the heavyweights.

    The modern NHL still has feared, dedicated enforcers like Mathieu Olivier, Ryan Reaves, Nicolas Deslauriers, and Arber Xhekaj. But when the playoffs arrive, they stay quiet. If the dedicated heavyweights aren't throwing punches, who is?

    The Accidental Gladiators

    The modern post-season fight is no longer a staged bout between policemen. It is a spontaneous clash between everyday players whose emotions boil over.
