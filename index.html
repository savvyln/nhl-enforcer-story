The Disappearing Playoff Enforcer

There is a spectrum of hockey fans. Those that hate fighting, and those that love it. We’re focusing in on those that love it.

For these hockey fight fanatics, the best time of year is playoffs. Over the past 5 years, 16 teams per season have faced off in over 425 games.

And in those 425+ games, fight fans have witnessed just 29 fights. Dance, Donnybrook, Drop the mitts, fisticuffs, have a go, puttin’ on the foil, snarl, tilt — whatever you call it, the quintessential playoff brawl is vanishing before our eyes.

While all 32 franchises battled through the regular seasons, only a select few dropped the gloves when the post-season pressure mounted.

As the game speeds up, the frequency of these clashes has plummeted from 13 fights in 2022-23 down to just 2 in 2024-25.

So, where is the action happening, and who is involved?

If you clicked through the fights above, you likely noticed a glaring absence: The Heavyweights.

The modern NHL still has feared, dedicated enforcers like Mathieu Olivier, Ryan Reaves, Nicolas Deslauriers, and Arber Xhekaj. But when the playoffs arrive, they stay quiet.

None of the league's top regular-season enforcers recorded a single playoff bout between 2021 and 2026. The modern post-season fight is no longer a staged bout between policemen; it is a spontaneous clash between highly skilled stars.
