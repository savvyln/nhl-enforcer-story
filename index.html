The Disappearing Playoff Enforcer

A deep dive into speed, skill, and the fading post-season brawl.

01 / DECLINE

The Icebreaker

There is a spectrum of hockey fans: those that hate fighting, and those that love it. For the fight fanatics, the ultimate stage is the post-season.

Yet over the last 5 years, across hundreds of intense playoff matchups, fans have witnessed a precipitous drop in total fights. The numbers plummeted from 10 fights in 2022-23 down to just 2 in 2024-25.

02 / THE MYTH

The Heavyweight Mirage

The modern NHL still has feared fighters like Mathieu Olivier, Ryan Reaves, and Nicolas Deslauriers. But when the playoffs arrive, where are they?

Try guessing how many fights the league's consensus toughest enforcers logged in our playoff dataset between 2021 and 2026.

03 / THE FIGHTERS

The Accidental Gladiators

If the dedicated heavyweights aren't throwing punches, who is?

Modern post-season bouts aren't staged brawls between enforcers. They are spontaneous clashes between elite scorers and top contributors whose emotions boil over in the heat of battle.

04 / THE REALITY

The Speed Trap

Why did the traditional enforcer vanish? Speed. Modern post-season hockey operates at an endurance sprint where seconds decide games.

With all-time bursts reaching 24.94 MPH and defensemen racking up over 4.15 miles in a single evening, a team cannot afford a roster spot for someone who can only drop the mitts.
